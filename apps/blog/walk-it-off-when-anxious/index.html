Anxious? Walk First, Think Second

When your mind won't stop racing, thinking harder rarely helps. Here's a simple ten-minute walk for stress and anxious thoughts.

📅 October 2026 ⏱ 4 min read ✦ Wellness & Habits

You know the feeling. Your chest is a bit tight, your thoughts are running laps, and every one of them starts with "what if".

So you do what most of us do. You sit there and try to think your way out of it. You make a plan, then a backup plan. Then you check your phone, which makes it worse.

Here's the thing: you can't reliably out-think a stressed body. But you can walk it.

Why thinking harder doesn't help

When you're stressed, your body is already on high alert. Your heart is quicker, your breathing is shallower, and you're full of energy with nowhere to put it.

Sitting still and ruminating keeps that energy bottled up. Your mind keeps scanning for the problem because your body is telling it there is one.

Movement gives that energy somewhere to go. It doesn't have to be a workout. A walk is enough.

The walk, step by step

Nothing clever here. That's the point.

01

Leave the phone in your pocket

If you can, leave notifications off too. This is ten minutes without the feed.

02

Walk slightly slower than feels natural

Anxious walking is fast and tense. Let your shoulders drop. Let your pace be a bit boring.

03

Give your thoughts one job

Pick something small to notice, like the colour of the doors on your street or the sound of your own footsteps. When the "what ifs" turn up, and they will, don't argue with them. Come back to the doors.

04

Say one true, calm thing

Not "everything is fine". Something you can actually believe, like "I've got through hard days before" or "I only need to deal with the next small thing."

05

Decide the next step when you're back

Not the whole plan. One thing. You'll make a better call at your front door than you did at your desk.

You don't have to fix the feeling

This isn't about making anxiety disappear in ten minutes. Some days a walk takes the edge off, and some days it just makes you feel slightly less stuck. Both count.

What it does is interrupt the loop. And once the loop is interrupted, everything else gets a bit easier to look at.

"You don't have to solve it on the walk. You just have to move it."

Make it easy to start

The hard part of walking when you're anxious is starting. That's where a timer helps: you're not committing to a long walk, just ten minutes.
